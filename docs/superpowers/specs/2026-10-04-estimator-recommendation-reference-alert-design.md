# Estimator recommendation alert based on the supplied reference

Date: 2026-10-04
Status: Revised specification approved by the user and implemented locally in Mode A on 2026-10-04

## Goal and current evidence

Make the estimator's recommendation alert visually follow the supplied reference: a pale mint card with a top icon/title/badge/close row, a concise explanation, a bordered two-column item comparison, and two equally prominent bottom actions. The user should understand what to add and why at a glance.

The current estimator renders a compact relationship sentence above the room's baskets. The current local implementation opens a wide right-side slide-out for its reference-style recommendation card. The user has now requested a **small centered modal instead**. `EstimatorRecommendations.tsx` has the selected source name, recommended main-line name, configured reason, required/optional setting, and a stable target ID. `LeadEstimateWorkspace.tsx` owns the existing rule that closing an automatically opened recommendation without selecting a related item unchecks the newly selected source. The current recommendation response and room view have no verified alternative relationship, selling-price comparison, savings percentage, or item image. The catalogue's optional base rate is not a customer-facing comparison price.

This builds on the approved [smart-recommendations specification](2026-10-03-estimator-smart-recommendations-design.md) and [plain-language alert specification](2026-10-03-estimator-plain-language-recommendation-alert-design.md).

## Scope and proposed behavior

- Present the recommendation as a **compact centered modal**, not a slider or right-side drawer. The latest user correction supersedes the earlier slide-out placement. Keep one complete alert card modeled on the screenshot. Do not surround it with a second visible generic dialog header or footer: the card itself has the title, close control, comparison, and actions.
- Match the reference's visual order and proportions: a small dark-green circular marker at top left; bold **Related item recommended** heading; a soft **Configuration suggestion** badge; a close control at top right; one or two lines of explanatory text; a fine-bordered comparison panel; then a two-button row. Use a plus cue between the two sides because the configured rule adds scope rather than replacing it. The close control must use the existing recommendation `onClose` handler.
- In the comparison panel, label the left side **Selected item** and the right side **Recommended addition**. Show the actual source and target main-line names with strong hierarchy. Use simple neutral letter tiles in the image positions when there is no item-specific image; never display an unrelated generic interior photo as though it depicts either product. Beneath the names, show the real configured reason if supplied. Do not display an amount or savings badge without a supported, comparable customer price.
- Show **one recommended main-line target at a time** in the modal. Put its current source name and target name in the paired panel, followed by the configured reason in one short paragraph when supplied. Do not repeat basket/sub-basket hierarchy, completion labels, or all available metadata in the primary card. For multiple eligible targets, show a compact position indicator and a way to move through them; after a successful selection, advance to the next pending target or a concise selected state. Preserve deduplication by stable target ID and the highest applicable required/optional priority. Keep supplementary guidance and unavailable/historical notes brief or collapsed so they do not turn the alert into a tall information panel.
- Put two equal-width actions immediately under the comparison panel, in the same arrangement as the reference: outlined **Not now** on the left and solid green **Add recommended item** on the right. The primary uses the current guarded `onSelect` path; the secondary uses the existing close path. When the modal opened automatically for a newly checked source, say plainly near that action that closing without choosing a related item will uncheck the source. Manual review closing keeps its existing behavior. A failed or stale selection must not appear selected.
- Keep the in-page alert compact above the estimator baskets, but restyle it in the same pale mint and dark green visual language. It continues to show the highest-priority pending relationship in plain words and opens the modal via **Review recommendations**. Do not fill the estimate page with full comparison cards.
- Use the screenshot's composition and emphasis, not its incompatible claims. These configured links are additions, so do not say **Alternative**, **Use Recommended** as a replacement, **Save 8%**, or **AI Suggestion**. Do not invent rates, savings, or product photos. Display real data only if the existing contract safely supplies it; the initial design does not require API changes or new media.
- Continue to support required and optional wording: required may say “{target} is needed for {source}”; optional says “Consider {target} for {source}.” Avoid “must be completed” status in the estimator. Keep unavailable, guidance-only, historical, loading, error, forbidden, stale, read-only, and all-selected states accurate without showing an enabled Add action where none is valid.

## UX and implementation constraints

Use the estimator's existing typography and green palette with a lightly tinted surface, fine borders, and clear button hierarchy. The modal should be visually compact: approximately 520–560 px maximum width on desktop, 16–20 px internal padding, a 16–18 px heading, 12–13 px explanation and item names, 10–11 px labels, and 12 px action text. These are visual targets, not a reason to clip content or reduce touch targets below 44 px. No harsh gradient, drop shadow, or sparkle icon is needed. The paired panel sits side by side when it fits and stacks on narrow screens. At 390 px the modal fits inside the viewport with internal scrolling only if needed; it must not become a full-screen sheet or introduce horizontal overflow. Long main-line names wrap without clipping. Use an accessible dialog with a labelled title, focus containment/return, Escape and backdrop close, visible focus, and keyboard operation. Respect reduced-motion preferences if a new transition is added.

The expected changes are frontend presentation using the existing `frontend/src/components/ui/Dialog.tsx` centered-dialog primitive, scoped CSS, and focused tests. Preserve the current automatic-versus-manual close context and the existing `onClose` and `onSelect` paths. The Dialog provides backdrop/Escape handling and focus containment/return; its default visible header and styling must be scoped so the recommendation has one visible header and a compact footprint. Preserve the recommendation API, Configuration rule semantics, permissions, saved estimate shape, amounts, save behavior, and source-uncheck safeguard. No dependency, data migration, or production action is expected. The worktree already contains approved, uncommitted recommendation changes; preserve those and unrelated edits.

## Risks and acceptance criteria

- For a configured POP false ceiling to painting link, the active alert shows POP false ceiling under **Selected item**, false ceiling painting under **Recommended addition**, and a truthful relationship sentence and configured reason. A functional-lights rule behaves the same way. Optional rules never claim the addition is required.
- The rendered **centered modal** visibly follows the screenshot's sequence: icon/title/badge/close, explanatory text, bordered paired item panel, and outlined/solid action pair. It remains compact at desktop and 390 px, with smaller type and spacing than the current wide slide-out. It does not show the old expanded list, separate **Why?** disclosures, duplicate dialog actions, or slide in from the edge.
- The modal shows a single target card at a time and provides access to every other eligible target. A Sub Basket target exposes its eligible main-line children as individually selectable targets. Shared targets have one Add action even when multiple sources recommend them.
- **Add recommended item** invokes the existing selection guard and accurately reflects success or failure. Closing an automatically opened modal without a selected linked item unchecks the newly checked source; manual review close does not. Outside click, Escape, close control, and secondary action follow the same close rule.
- No fabricated price, percentage, photo, substitute relationship, or AI provenance appears. Loading, stale, permission, unavailable, read-only, and empty states remain truthful and usable.
- Focused interaction tests cover the paired data, required/optional copy, multiple-target navigation, selection, close/uncheck behavior, and exceptional states. Rendered desktop and mobile checks cover visual hierarchy, wrapping, focus, and horizontal overflow. Frontend typecheck/build and repository hygiene checks pass.

## Assumption for approval

The latest request places the reference-style card in a **small centered modal**, while the compact in-page alert remains its entry point. Lisno's configured links are **add-on recommendations**, so the card uses addition wording and neutral item tiles where product-specific photos are unavailable. Approval confirms this revised placement and density, including the omission of unverified price savings.
